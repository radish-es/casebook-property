### Hypothetical Facts

Not since the forges and factories of the industrial revolution have large industrial developments provoked as much backlash in communities as the gigantic data centres now being planned and built across Canada (and elsewhere in the world). Legal claims to slow or prevent these developments are on the rise, as local residents argue that property and people are increasingly under threat.

Review the facts reported in [this article published by *The Guardian*](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/28/datacenters-sound-pollution-lawsuits) newspaper. In addition to those facts, suppose that, hypothetically, the residents complaining of noise from DataOne had moved nearby recently and were attracted by development of the land as a new golf course, though some of them were aware even then that the course may close to make way for a new data centre. While the DataOne development is currently located on the outskirts of town where there are mostly cornfields and cow sheds, the Vineland town council has announced that it is examining the amendment of its local land use laws to accommodate more data centres in this area in the near future. 

Quiet Communities Inc. has started looking into the title records concerning the land on which DataOne has built its Vineland centre. They've discovered that there is what appears to be a restrictive covenant attached to DataOne's title stipulating that the land only be used for "sustainable farming" (a small plot on the land is apparently where DataOne plans to produce the "weekly baskets of organic, non-GMO fruits and vegetables" mentioned in the article). The covenant existed on title at the time when DataOne purchased the land from the farmer who owned it. The farmer had started to operate a golf course on the land in 2024 but quickly abandoned the business. The vendor (farmer) had mentioned in passing to DataOne's lawyers when they were negotiating to purchase the land that there were some "sustainability constraints" around using the land but did not elaborate. 

### The Problem

**What legal claims, if any, might local residents bring against DataOne to address the problems described in the *Guardian* article, what are the chances that those claims will succeed, and what would be the likely result?**

For the purposes of answering this problem, assume that all relevant facts take place in Canada.
